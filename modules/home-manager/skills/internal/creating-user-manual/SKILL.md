# Creating a Personal User Manual

## Overview

A user manual accelerates trust by making implicit preferences explicit. It reduces the guesswork in working relationships and prevents misunderstandings from derailing collaboration.

**Core insight:** The goal isn't comprehensive self-documentation. It's friction reduction.

## When to Use

- Creating a new user manual from scratch
- Updating an existing user manual
- Helping someone discover their working patterns through self-reflection
- Adapting a manual for different audiences (ICs vs managers)

## Essential Sections

Based on Jay Desai's framework (PatientPing CEO) and Atlassian's team playbook:

### 1. Communication Preferences
- **Channel hierarchy:** Most to least urgent (Call > Text > Slack > Email)
- **Response expectations:** "I like quick 'got it' acknowledgments"
- **After-hours policy:** CRITICAL - "I may send messages at night but never expect immediate responses"

### 2. Working Hours & Availability
- Typical hours and timezone
- Protected time (focus blocks, family time)
- How to reach you urgently

### 3. What Motivates You
- What work energizes you
- What you find meaningful

### 4. Triggers (What Drives You Nuts)
Be specific. Not "poor communication" but:
- "Having to ask about something twice"
- "Surprises with bad news"
- "Meetings without agendas"

### 5. Feedback Preferences
**Two separate sections:**
- How you GIVE feedback (timing, format, directness)
- How you RECEIVE feedback (best format, what helps you hear it)

### 6. When Mistakes Happen
CRITICAL for psychological safety:
- "Come to me early with bad news"
- "I won't shoot the messenger"
- "I will be frustrated if I'm surprised"

### 7. Weaknesses (And How Team Can Help)
Vulnerability builds trust. Be specific:
- The weakness
- How it manifests
- What teammates can do

### 8. First 90 Days (For Managers)
What you expect from new hires:
- Ask questions constantly
- Over-communicate
- Share your learning

## Audience Adaptation

### For Managers
Include:
- 1:1 structure and cadence
- Delegation philosophy
- Performance review approach
- How you handle micromanagement tendencies

### For ICs (Staff+)
Include:
- "How I can help you" (your role is ambiguous to others)
- Current focus areas (since you span teams)
- How to get time with you (no default channel exists)

Skip manager-specific sections (1:1s, performance reviews).

## Self-Reflection Process

When someone doesn't know their patterns, use these:

### The Instant Irritation Test
"Which bothers you most?"
- Messages without context
- Meetings that could be emails
- Being asked the same thing twice
- Last-minute changes

### Reverse Engineering Triggers
- "What do you silently judge others for?"
- "What did a past colleague do that still annoys you?"

### Complete the Sentence
- "I do my best work when my manager..."
- "The fastest way to lose my trust is..."
- "Please don't assume I..."

### Check Blind Spots
Ask 2-3 trusted colleagues:
> "What took you a while to figure out about working with me?"

## Validation Step

**CRITICAL: Before sharing broadly:**

1. Share draft with 2-3 close colleagues
2. Ask: "Would you recognize this person? What's missing? What's wrong?"
3. Iterate based on feedback
4. Revisit every 6 months

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Writing aspirationally | Ask "Would teammates recognize this?" |
| Only listing strengths | For each strength, include the shadow side |
| Being too vague | "Poor communication frustrates me" -> "Having to ask twice" |
| Writing once, forgetting | Schedule 6-month review |
| Performative manual | Your behavior must match your document |

## The Performative Trap

**Warning:** A user manual sets expectations your behavior must match.

If you write "I value directness" but get defensive when criticized, the manual backfires. Your team will test whether the document is real.

**Test yourself:** For each preference you list, recall a recent situation where you actually demonstrated it. If you can't, reconsider including it.

## Minimum Viable Manual

If overwhelmed, start with just:

1. One thing that helps me do great work
2. One thing that frustrates me
3. One thing people often misread about me

That's a user manual. Everything else is refinement.
